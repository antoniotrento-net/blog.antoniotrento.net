In every company there are documents that move — or that should move. A case that passes from one person to another, a quote that becomes an order and then an invoice, a contract that expires, a report that has to be found on the spot. And in too many companies these flows are made of email inboxes, shared folders, Excel sheets and people's memory: the case stuck for three weeks because nobody knew it was their turn, the PDF unfindable among two thousand others, the margin lost in the transfer between quote and invoice. This guide collects the articles on how you make **documents and workflows move**: dossiers that advance instead of bogging down, information that gets found instead of hunted, data that sits on a single thread instead of in separate drawers.

## The principle: one thread, not separate drawers

The thread that ties these articles is that **company documents are not an archive, they are a flow.** The value isn't "having" the documents — you already have too many lists and folders — it's that at the right moment the right information is found, that the case advances to the next state, that the data isn't retyped by hand from one system to another losing pieces along the way. When contracts, quotes, cases and communications live in separate drawers that don't talk to each other, each one tells a different story and the glue is people's time. When they sit on **one thread**, the system *knows* on its own where everything stands.

The other thread is the honest role of **AI**: here it serves as an engine that *finds the information in your real material and cites the source* — not as a tool that invents content or decides in your place. AI lets you fish the right piece among your documents; it doesn't write you a truth you don't have.

## The piece that decides everything: state and permissions

Under every document flow that works sit two things. The first is **state**: where every case, every document, every deadline stands — because state is what removes the phone calls ("where are we?") and stops things from stalling without anyone noticing. The second is **permissions**: who sees what. In a serious flow not everything is for everyone — admin sees the money, sales sees the relationship, each person their own — and permissions aren't only there to protect the confidential, they're there so people aren't scattered across material that doesn't concern them.

Then there's a principle that comes back in almost every article: **two views on the same data.** Admin and sales, office and client, look at the same document looking for different things — and you need different views on the same thread, not two separate systems that someone then has to keep aligned by hand. That's where, in the "translation" from one department to another, the margin is lost and cases stall.

## Why you need a single direction

Making documents move means holding together the data (the contracts, the price lists, the materials), the backend (the states, the rules, the hook into the gestionale) and the frontend (the views for whoever works it and the portal for whoever consults). If different vendors do these pieces, they blame each other over where the data got lost in the transfer, and you pay in months and errors. One pair of hands builds the whole thread, from the document to the state to the invoice, without the seams where things get lost — the same logic that runs through the [guide to buying custom software]({{ '/en/pillar/buying-custom-software/' | relative_url }}).
